## ==What are they and why do they matter?==
Tell me when... or give me an example of... type of questions intend to instigate the candidate to craft deep stories to understand if that person align his core competencies with what the organization is looking for.

==Teamwork and Collaboration==
==Cultural Fit==
==Problem-Solving Beyond Code==
==Resilience and Adaptability==

**Most problems in software engineering teams are people problems, rather than software problems**
"How do you manage difficult stakeholders?"
"How do you manage ambiguous situations or ambiguous tasks?"
"How do you handle breaking production code? Do you bounce back when things go wrong or you keep calm?"
"Tell me about a time when you had a conflict with a coworker"
"Tell me about a recent failure and what you learned form the experience"
"Give me and example of a time when you faced a challenge on a project and how you solved it"

==The further you go in your career, the more ambiguous tasks you have to tackle==.

Even if you don't provide deep answers to behavioral questions, the interviewer goal is to probe your story looking for some competency or value that aligns with the company, so it's important to have meaningful stories and avoid surface level responses, because shallow responses are gonna get dug, and if you don't know how to go beyond the surface, your confidence is gonna be challenged.

The whole point of behavioral interviews is to be able to predict future behavior from the candidate.

### Crafting good stories
- Help interviewers relate to you in a more personal level.
- A good crafted story generates more engagement from the interviewers in the interview and can make it become the moment super comfortable for you.
- Allows you to demonstrate your competences
- STAR - Situation, Task, Action, Result. Build your stories using this framework.
- To bond emotionally with the interviewer, focus on the emotional journey. Showing how the challenges shaped me.
#### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- Too much detail
- Not clearly defining your role
- Overemphasizing the problem
- Sounding rehearsed or scripted

## SWOT Analysis

- Strengths - skills, experiences, and qualities that you have
	- "I've been building C# applications for..."
	- "I've found myself in team lead positions..."
- Weaknesses - areas for improvement (skill, language, or tech stack to learn)
	- "I've not yet spent time with Maui but I'm excited that..."
	- "I had some projects where I over-abstracted but I learned that..."
- Opportunities - Industries trends, company directions,
	- This does require solid research
	- Be honest but align with your goals
	- "I'm excited that the company is investing more into AI solutions because..."
- Threats - External factors that may interfere with your job search
	- "I realize there are a lot of developers using X, so I wanted to also skill up in Y and Z"

## Nailing the Interview
- First impression is powerful, usually sets the tone for the rest of the interview
- Facial expressions, body language, everything matters
- Usually interviews begin with tell me a bit about yourself (I tend to go for a happy an enthusiastic view of myself and my story)
- Don't be afraid to ask guidance to the interviewer on what to focus on your responses. If you feel comfortable doing so, that can be a good aspect. Also is always good to confirm verbally or non-verbally (through expressions) if the interviewer is understanding everything

### Dealing With Difficult Questions
- Pause... and think. Don't rush to answer immediately. Gather your thought first.
- If you're caught off guard, acknowledge and indicate you're thinking of an appropriate answer.
- Be honest when you don't know the answer. Transparency is a sign of maturity and self-awareness. If you don't yet have such experience or never worked with that technology, you can put things like ==***"Hey, yes, I've not done this before, or I don't have experience doing this, is it okay if I tell you about how I believe that I would navigate this type of situation?"***== That is a great way to showcase how you can handle limitations in real time and it's also an opportunity to show your critical thinking about how to deal with new situations.
- Failure can be positive opportunities. Don't leave things at the failure point -- Go beyond. Some people just look someone to put the blame on, but when you stop when you recognize where things might have gone wrong, you miss the opportunity to grow, and giving a sign to a coworker that failure has no value for you.

## Through a manager's lens
### Warning Signs
- Steering away from the main point of the question
	- Occurs when and interviewee avoids a question
	- May indicate a candidate is not prepared or does not have experience to answer
	- Focus on the goal of the question - You can acknowledge if you don't have that experience *yet*
- Vague or short answers
- Not clarifying questions
	- If you are unsure of what the interviewer is asking you should ask for clarity
- Focus on the negative too much
- Not reflecting on mistakes and failures
	- If you end the story and that's it, you missed the opportunity to show a growth mindset
- Being the hero or lone wolf
	- Usually when someone stands out technically or emotionally in a particular team, this one cannot focus on how bad everyone was, and how he used to incorporate all the work and was the only salvation of the project
### Red flags
 - Dishonest or exaggerating experience
 - Discriminatory behavior
 - Unprofessional behavior or attitude
 - Violating confidentiality
 - Lack of respect for the company's core values

## Common Questions
### Problem Solving Abilities
- "Tell me about a time when you faced an unexpected challenge at work. How did you handle it?"
	- 1st - Set the scene and explain why is that scenario a fit to an unexpected challenge
	- 2nd - How you address the challenge? Did you just jump in and fix the solution without much assessment or thought, if that's the case, explain why? Or was it something more deep and you had to analyze the situation to identify the root cause?
- "Describe a time when you had to solve a problem under tight deadlines. How did you manage your time and prioritize tasks"
	- What to do first? and why?
	- Why was the deadline tight? Was it external demands? Was it project resources?
	- What challenges you faced?
	- What lessons did you learned?
- "Give me and example of a time when you identified a problem before it became serious. What steps did you take?" (wants to understand how you structure proactive actions)
	- Did you speak to a manager? Did you speak to a tech lead? Did you just go and fixed the problem?
- "Tell me about a time when you had to solve a difficult problem with limited information or resources" (want to assess creativity, resourcefulness, and adaptability)
	- Why information was limited? Why resources were limited?
	- Was it data constraint? logging constraint?
	- Did you consult any experts in the team?
	- Did you made educated guesses and assumptions based on what you knew already?

### Teamwork and Collaboration
- "Tell me about a time when you had to collaborate with a colleague who had a different work approach or opinion. How did you manage to work together?"
	- "What was the difference over? Difference on opinion, approach, work style?"
	- "How did you manage potential conflicts and communication?"

- "Describe a time when you worked on a team project and faced a setback (lost of progress). How did your team overcome it?"
	- Describe the setback being honest
		- Was it resource constraint, an interpersonal conflict?
		- It could be a missed deadline
	- Did you suggest new solutions? Did you facilitate tasks? Did you act as a broker between team members that had different opinions?

- "Describe a situation where you successfully worked as part of a team to achieve a goal. What was your role, and how did you contribute?"
	- Were you a contributor? Were you leading the team?
	- How was task prioritization? How was communication?

- "Tell me a time when you received feedback from a team member. How did you handle it, and how did it improve your work?"
	- How did you response to that?

### Leadership
- "Tell me about a time when you had to lead a project or a team. How did you ensure the team was motivated and on track?"
	- How you motivated people?
	- How you inspire when challenge comes?
	- Celebrating?

- "Tell me about a time when you had to give a constructive feedback to a teammate. How did you approach it? What was the outcome?"
	- How was the issue? behavior? performance?
	- How did you approach?
	- How was the outcome

- "Give me and example of a time when you took the initiative to improve a process or solve a problem?"
	- Was it a specific task? Was it a process? Was it a team dynamic issue?
	- What was your initiative?
	- How was your outcome?

- "Tell me about a time when you had to make a decision without having complete information. How did you approach it?"
	- How do you lead yourself and other through ambiguity.
	- How was the issue? Shifting circumstances? Lack of data? Time constraints? Not enough info? What was the pros and cons? Talked to others?

### Communication
- "Tell me about a situation where effective communication made a significant difference in the outcome of a project."
	- How do you adjust communication to be effective?

- "How do you ensure your communication is clear and understood by all team members?"
	- Trying to assess awareness on your own communication

- "How do you handle situations where there is a communication breakdown within your team?"
	- Communication challenges are one of the biggest issues in software engineering

- "Describe a situation where you had to persuade others to see your point of view?"

### Customer Focus
- "Tell me about a situation where customer feedback influenced your work. How did you incorporate it?"
	- Even as a back-end developer, how do you serve other parts of the company wisely?

- "Give an example of a time when you had to balance technical requirements with customer satisfaction?"

### Other competencies
- "Give me and example of a project where unexpected technical issues required to change your approach"
- "Give an example of how you've handled learning a new tool or technology that wasn't intuitive"
- "Give an example of a time when you improved an existing process or tool to make it more efficient"
